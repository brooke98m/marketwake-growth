The honest diagnosis

The current model
was built for yesterday.

We have built something real. But the structure that got us here, teams organized by discipline, work siloed by channel, selling tactics instead of outcomes, will not scale to $50M. Not because the people are wrong. Because the architecture is.

01
We sell ingredients, not meals
Our service pages list SEO, Paid Media, Social, Content as separate things clients can buy. That trains clients to think transactionally, and trains our teams to work in silos. Nobody owns the outcome. Everybody owns a channel.
02
Career paths hit a ceiling early
There is one Creative Director for all design. One senior content strategist for all copy. One SEO lead for all organic. Every talented specialist eventually looks up and sees only one seat above them, often already filled. So they leave.
03
Traffic and ops cannot scale heroically
Right now, our traffic manager must understand every discipline to make good staffing calls. Our ops team fills gaps manually. Our AMs run accounts differently. None of this is sustainable at double the headcount.
04
The generalist trap drains everyone
When a designer works on web, social, paid, and presentations for the same client, they never develop mastery in any one area. The same is true across every discipline. We are wide. We need to be deep.

02
The model debate

Account-based pods
or offering-based pods?

The instinct to build autonomous, self-directed teams that operate like mini-agencies is exactly right. The question is what we organize them around. Account-based pods are built around who we serve. Offering-based pods are built around what we deliver. One is fragile. One compounds.

Client-based pods
Built around who you serve, not what you deliver
You cannot size a pod around clients you cannot control. Client A churns at month 8. Client B doubles scope. Every client event forces a restructure.
Generalist pods produce generalist work. A designer doing web, social, and pitch decks never develops mastery in any of them.
Career paths collapse. One designer per pod means one ceiling per pod, a generalist MD above, not a craft leader.
Knowledge is trapped in the pod MD. When they leave, client context leaves with them.
Innovation scatters. Pod A builds differently from Pod B. Nothing compounds. The wheel is rebuilt every time.
"Pods focused around accounts/clients are nearly impossible to sustain because of the ever-changing nature of clients.", Sammy, Vision Exercise
Outcome-based offering teams
Built around what you deliver, not who you serve
The Web Growth team's size is set by how much web work exists, not by which clients are in it. Clients change. The offering is stable.
Deep specialization is enforced by structure. The social creative on the Social Growth team makes only social creative, every day. Mastery follows.
Career paths multiply. Every offering has its own CD, its own Director. Multiple people can hold those titles simultaneously without anyone being wrong.
Client knowledge is distributed. The AM owns the relationship. The offering liaison brings delivery expertise. Either can change without disrupting the other.
Collective intelligence compounds. The Web Growth team builds every website. Their frameworks and standards get better with every engagement.
The offering model is what makes this scalable. Autonomous teams, strong leads, operating independently. Same ambition. Much stronger foundation.

The mindset

The Brain Trust.
How we show up.

Inspired by Pixar's Brain Trust from Creativity, Inc., this partnership only works if you bring radical candor, healthy tension, and a refusal to settle. Here is what that looks like in practice.

01
Speak up and push back
If something is not right, say it. If a process is broken, flag it. If the standard is slipping, call it out. Silence is the enemy of quality. Your voice is the reason you are in this role.
02
Facilitate healthy debate
The best ideas come from friction, not consensus. Challenge assumptions, stress-test ideas, and push back when something does not feel right. The goal is not agreement, it is the best answer.
03
Never accept "good enough"
Do not settle for fear of stepping on toes. If the work can be better, push for better. Politeness is not a reason to ship something that does not meet the standard. Be kind, but be honest.
04
Fight for best in class
Every deliverable, every process, every standard should be something you are proud to put your name on. Best in class is not a slogan. It is the filter you run every decision through.
05
Represent your discipline with pride
How you show up, how you communicate, how you advocate for your discipline — that matters. You are the expert voice. Bring that perspective with confidence and conviction.
